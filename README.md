## The entry

> When the rice cooker clicked off at dusk, my grandmother lifted the lid so the steam fogged my glasses, then pressed the warmest spoonful into my bowl with her garlic-smelling hands and whispered, eat — you are still growing.

The memory is a small evening ritual in my grandmother's kitchen: the rice cooker clicking off at dusk, the cloud of steam, the warmest spoonful saved for me. Nothing grand happens — that is the point. The details are the ones a child actually remembers: fogged glasses, hands that smell of garlic, a whisper that treats dinner as care. The ache comes from the last words, "you are still growing" — a sentence only a grandmother says, and one you only understand years later, when the kitchen is quiet and she is gone.
